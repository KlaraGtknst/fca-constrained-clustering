\section{Conclusion, Limitations, and Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

Our study provides two main contributions. 
First, we showed that \ac{mlb} constraints can be integrated natively into the \ac{fca} framework through a fixpoint-based closure operator. 
Second, experiments on the BankSearch dataset demonstrated that exact consistency between expert-defined hierarchies and data-driven topic models is rarely observed in real-world corpora, motivating the need for relaxed coherence-based integration strategies.

The consistency-lattice retains only concepts whose extents coincide exactly in both the \ac{mlb}-constrained and the topic model-based lattices. 
In our experiments, only trivial concepts were shared exactly, indicating that strict consistency is often too restrictive for heterogeneous knowledge bases.

In principle, pruning low-support concepts that could potentially form exact overlaps can yield an incomplete consistency-lattice. 
However, the empty consistency-lattice in our experiments primarily reflects semantic differences between the two knowledge bases rather than an artefact of pruning: 
The \ac{mlb}-constrained lattice is derived from manual annotations and thus reflects human expertise and annotation decisions, whereas the topic model-based lattice is data-driven and captures latent statistical regularities in the corpus. 

Instead of requiring identical extents, the coherence-lattice identifies partially overlapping document clusters and thereby enables the analysis of meaningful semantic alignments between heterogeneous knowledge bases. 
% Moreover, the number of documents contained in coherence-lattice concepts provides an indication of the degree of agreement between the underlying knowledge bases.

Future work will investigate the integration of more than two knowledge bases, methods for selecting similarity thresholds that yield coherence-lattices of comparable size, and alternative overlap measures beyond Jaccard similarity for comparing concept extents across multiple lattices.
